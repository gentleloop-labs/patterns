## Voice and safety rules

- Address the user consistently within each language. Brazilian Portuguese
  uses `você`; German and French use the polite form; neutral Spanish uses
  `tú`; Japanese avoids an explicit pronoun where natural.
- Describe saved facts and user choices. Do not grade a practice as successful
  or failed, promise improvement, prescribe treatment, or turn a score into a
  clinical conclusion.
- Keep `self-check`, `snapshot`, `not a diagnosis`, and `not emergency
  support` together anywhere Y-BOCS results could otherwise look diagnostic.
- Use `local emergency services` without inventing a country-specific number.
- Tips are optional and unlock no features. Pro is a one-time unlock; never
  call it a subscription.
- Personal content remains on the device unless the user manually exports it.
  A manual JSON/ZIP/PDF export is unencrypted. Optional anonymous usage
  analytics remain off until enabled and exclude journal/OCD content,
  generated summaries, locale, and language choice.

## Terminology references

- Brazil's Ministry of Health uses `Transtorno Obsessivo-Compulsivo (TOC)`;
  Brazilian clinical research uses `Exposição e Prevenção de Resposta`.
- Germany's federal health portal uses `Zwangsstörung`; the independent
  evidence portal Gesundheitsinformation.de uses `Reizkonfrontation mit
  Reaktionsverhinderung`.
- Japan's Ministry of Health uses `強迫性障害（強迫症）` and
  `曝露反応妨害法（ERP）`.
- Spain's Ministry of Health uses `Trastorno Obsesivo Compulsivo (TOC)` and
  `exposición con prevención de respuesta`.
- France's public health-insurance service uses `trouble obsessionnel
  compulsif (TOC)`; Haute Autorité de Santé uses `exposition avec prévention
  de la réponse (EPR)`.

The corresponding source links and the date consulted are retained in each
locale's review record.
