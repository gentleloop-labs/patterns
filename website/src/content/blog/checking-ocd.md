<p class="lead">
Checking OCD is a presentation in which compulsions centre on verifying that something is safe, correct, or done: the stove, the locks, the emails you sent, the words you just said. There is a well-replicated experimental finding at the heart of it that is genuinely useful to know: repeated checking does not strengthen your memory of having checked. It reliably weakens your confidence in that memory, which is why the tenth check feels less convincing than the first.
</p>

## What gets checked

- **Safety.** Stoves, ovens, irons, hair straighteners, candles, taps, plugs, heaters.
- **Security.** Doors, windows, cars, alarms, safes.
- **Harm avoidance.** Retracing a drive to confirm you did not hit anyone. This overlaps with [harm OCD](/blog/harm-ocd-intrusive-thoughts).
- **Correctness.** Re-reading emails, re-counting figures, verifying you did not make a mistake at work.
- **Communication.** Replaying what you said to check it was not offensive or misleading.
- **Body checking.** Examining a mole, a lump, a sensation.
- **Mental checking.** Reviewing a memory to establish what happened, without any physical checking at all. See [mental compulsions](/blog/pure-o-mental-compulsions).

The last one matters, because someone who has stopped walking back upstairs but is still mentally reviewing the stove has not stopped checking.

## The memory distrust finding

This is the part worth understanding properly.

Marcel van den Hout and Merel Kindt ran a series of experiments in the early 2000s in which participants without OCD repeatedly checked a virtual gas stove. After repeated checking, participants' **memory accuracy was unaffected**. What changed was the *quality* of the recollection: it became less vivid, less detailed, and participants were markedly less confident in it.

The proposed mechanism is that repetition breeds familiarity, and familiarity shifts processing from detailed and perceptual to conceptual and automatic. You stop encoding this particular instance of looking at the stove, because you have looked at the stove three hundred times, and they blur.

The consequence for OCD is direct and slightly brutal. The compulsion designed to produce certainty is the thing manufacturing the uncertainty. Each check degrades the recollection a little more, which produces a stronger urge to check, which degrades it further.

Notably this happened in participants **without** OCD. It is not a defect specific to people with the disorder. It is what checking does to anyone. People with OCD simply do far more of it.

## Why "just remember properly" does not work

People with checking OCD are frequently told to pay more attention, or to say "the stove is off" out loud, or to take a photo. These help briefly and then get absorbed.

The photo becomes something to check. The spoken phrase acquires rules about whether it was said with enough attention. Anything used to generate certainty gets recruited as a compulsion, because the underlying problem is not a lack of evidence. It is an unwillingness to accept a normal level of doubt, and no amount of evidence resolves that.

This is why [ERP](/blog/what-is-erp-exposure-response-prevention) does not try to help you remember better. It works on your relationship with doubt.

## How ERP treats checking

- **Check once, deliberately, then leave.** One good look, with attention, and then no return regardless of how it feels. The rule is set in advance so it is not renegotiated under distress.
- **Delay the return check.** If leaving outright is not achievable, [delay it](/blog/compulsion-delay-technique). Five minutes, then twenty, then an hour, then the whole day.
- **Do not use memory aids.** No photos, no lists, no ritual phrases. They become compulsions.
- **Prevent the mental version.** No reviewing on the train. This is usually the hardest part and it is where progress stalls if it is missed.
- **Add deliberate uncertainty.** Leaving the house without checking at all, on purpose, as an exposure. Or leaving after checking in a slightly sloppy way so the doubt is guaranteed.
- **Include reassurance.** No asking a partner whether they saw you lock it. See [reassurance seeking](/blog/reassurance-seeking-ocd).

A common structure is to start with a lower-stakes target - an email, a light switch - rather than the stove, and to build up through a [hierarchy](/blog/how-to-build-an-exposure-hierarchy).

## Responsibility and the "just in case"

Checking is closely tied to inflated responsibility: the belief that you are uniquely accountable for preventing harm, and that failing to prevent it is equivalent to causing it. Paul Salkovskis's cognitive model of OCD puts this at the centre.

The practical form is "it only takes one second to check, and if I am wrong the consequences are catastrophic." The asymmetry looks compelling, and it is the sentence that funds the entire ritual. It is worth noticing that it does not survive contact with reality: it never was one second, it never was one check, and the catastrophe has not been averted by any of them.

## What this looks like from the inside

The straighteners were mine. I would look at them, see them unplugged, walk to the door, and by the time I reached the door the image in my head had gone soft. I could not tell whether I was remembering this morning or the four hundred previous mornings, because they had all merged into a single generic memory of looking at a plug socket.

That was the thing the research explained for me and nothing else had. I had assumed my memory was defective and that this was why I had to check. It was the other way round. I had been sanding down my own recollection with repetition for years, and then treating the resulting vagueness as proof I needed to sand harder.

Stopping was awful for about two weeks. Leaving the house genuinely felt like an act of negligence, and I had to accept that if the flat burned down it would be, in some sense, because I chose not to check. That is not a comfortable position and ERP does not pretend otherwise. It asks you to hold it anyway.

Nothing burned down. It never does, but that is not really the point either, because a run of good outcomes is just more evidence and OCD is not short of evidence. What changed was that the doubt stopped requiring anything from me.
