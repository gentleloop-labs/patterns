## Post 1 — Meet Patterns

Patterns is a quieter place to notice what keeps repeating.

Write a daily journal entry, record an OCD event, add a distress rating, and return later with more context—not more judgment.

Patterns is for personal reflection and self-tracking. It does not diagnose, treat, prevent, or cure any condition, and it is not a replacement for care from a qualified clinician.

## Post 2 — The daily loop

Notice → choose → record → reflect.

That is the whole loop. You do not need a perfect streak or a perfectly written entry. A small record can be enough to help you remember the moment and understand it later.

## Post 3 — Why Patterns

Some thoughts deserve a private place.

Patterns is local-first: your journal entries and OCD records stay on your device. There is no account to create and no automatic upload of what you write. You decide what leaves your device.

Private. Calm. Intentional.

## Post 4 — NF on living with OCD

After the *Perception* tour, NF said he entered therapy in a deeply difficult mental state and received an OCD diagnosis. He has since put parts of that experience into his music: rumination, thoughts that keep circling, repetitive responses, and the way obsession can make finishing creative work feel impossible.

His story is not a neat before-and-after. It is a reminder that OCD can be internal, disruptive, and hard to see—and that asking for help can be part of the story.

## Post 5 — Howie Mandel on living with OCD

Howie Mandel has spoken for years about contamination fears, avoidance, and the effect OCD had on his family before he received a diagnosis in his 40s. He also speaks plainly about what helps him: openness, family, professional support, medication, and persistence when the first option is not the right fit.

OCD is not shorthand for being neat. It can be a restrictive cycle of intrusive fear, rituals, avoidance, and short-lived relief.
